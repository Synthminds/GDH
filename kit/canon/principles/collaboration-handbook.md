# Design Collaboration Handbook (Design Better)

Book id `collaboration-handbook`, id prefix `P-COL`. 31 principles, 7 of them gates. The machine-readable copy is in [principles.json](../principles.json); this file matches it word for word.

Gates: P-COL-03, P-COL-06, P-COL-16, P-COL-17, P-COL-20, P-COL-26, P-COL-27.
Every other principle is a default. Query them with the `design-principles` skill.

## Chapter 1: Raising your collaborative intelligence

**P-COL-01 Borrow methods from other fields.** When the usual design method stalls, borrow a working method from a field outside design, such as improv, science or comedy writing, and name where it came from. Test: The exploration notes name at least one method or reference from outside the project's own field.

## Chapter 2: Collaborative leadership

**P-COL-02 Restate their view first.** Before disagreeing with a user preference, restate their view accurately in their own terms, then offer your option. Test: Each disagreement reply opens with an accurate restatement of the user's position.

**P-COL-03 Hand off the why.** Put the goal, audience and constraint behind a task into every handoff and sub-agent prompt, and leave the how open where you can. Test: Every handoff note and delegation prompt holds a why-this-matters line.

**P-COL-04 Ask what they mean.** Meet a surprising or unclear idea during creation with a clarifying question instead of a judgment. Test: No reply in an explore round rejects an idea before a clarifying question about it.

**P-COL-05 Build on their idea.** In each new iteration, keep or extend at least one element the user proposed and name it. Test: Each iteration after feedback names at least one user-proposed element it keeps or extends.

**P-COL-06 No hidden favorite.** Present options with equal care and detail, and label your recommendation separately from the descriptions. Test: Option descriptions have parallel structure and the recommendation sits in its own labelled block.

**P-COL-07 Provoke when a round stalls.** When a round produces no directional feedback, add one clearly labelled provocation that breaks a stated assumption of the brief. Test: After a round with no directional feedback, the next round contains one labelled provocation option.

## Chapter 3: Understanding your colleagues

**P-COL-08 Know the collaborator's goal.** At kickoff, learn who you are working with, what they are trying to achieve and which metric they answer to, then design toward the overlap with the design goal. Test: The brief names the collaborator's metric and at least one design decision that serves it.

**P-COL-09 Agree who owns what.** Before work starts, list what the agent decides, what the user decides and what belongs to others such as copy, engineering or legal. Test: Every decision in the record has an owner taken from the agreed role list.

**P-COL-10 Draw from distant references.** Build each reference set from at least three distinct domains or styles, and keep dissenting views in the record instead of deleting them. Test: The board or option set draws on three or more distinct domains and the record keeps minority views.

**P-COL-11 Mirror the real audience.** Check that imagery, names, sample data and copy reflect the documented audience rather than the people making the work. Test: A review lists the deliverable's people, names and sample data against the documented audience with mismatches noted.

## Chapter 4: Understanding your customers

**P-COL-12 Ask the worst case.** At kickoff, ask what the worst outcome of the design could be and for whom, and give each risk a mitigation or an explicit acceptance. Test: The brief has a worst-case list with affected groups and a mitigation or acceptance for each.

**P-COL-13 Trust what people do.** Tag every piece of feedback as observed or stated, and follow observed behavior when the two conflict. Test: Feedback items carry an observed or stated tag and conflicts follow the observed item.

**P-COL-14 Ask about the artifact in view.** Ask questions about the artifact in front of the person, not about hypotheticals or memory. Test: Review questions refer to the shown artifact, with none asking for prediction or recall.

**P-COL-15 Pair numbers with reasons.** Back any data-justified decision with both a behavioral number and an observed reason, or state which one is missing. Test: Each data-backed decision cites a number and a reason, or names the missing half.

## Chapter 5: Mission, vision, and purpose

**P-COL-16 Write a three-line POV.** Write a point of view for each project with three labelled lines: the specific person served, their deep unmet need, and why that is a real insight. Test: The brief holds User, Need and Insight lines, and the need line names no feature.

**P-COL-17 Success means do and feel.** Define success with at least one measure of what people do and one target for what they should feel. Test: The success criteria hold at least one behavior measure and one feeling target.

**P-COL-18 Mission is now, vision is later.** Keep a present-tense mission of what the work does now and why separate from a future-tense vision of where it is going. Test: The brand docs hold two distinct statements, and each review records a mission yes or no with evidence.

**P-COL-19 Tell the design's story.** Give every presented concept a two or three sentence story of what it is and why someone would use it. Test: Each presented concept carries a short story that a non-designer can restate.

## Chapter 6: Creativity, innovation, and divergent thinking

**P-COL-20 Diverge before converging.** Label each phase diverge or converge, and run a diverge phase before any single direction is refined on an open-ended request. Test: The plan labels each phase, and an open-ended project shows a diverge phase before refinement.

**P-COL-21 Defer the decision.** Explore materials and options for longer and commit to a composition later than feels comfortable. Test: The decision record dates the choice after three or more materially different directions.

**P-COL-22 Draft your own first.** Draft your own options before you judge someone else's references or assets. Test: In reviews of outside references, the log shows the agent's own alternatives drafted first.

**P-COL-23 Generate options independently.** Produce early options in separate passes that do not see each other, under a fixed time or count budget, before any comparison. Test: Early options were generated independently and their count matches the stated budget.

**P-COL-24 Vote in named categories.** Converge by picking a winner in named categories such as easiest win, most delightful and moon shot, rather than one overall favorite. Test: The shortlist shows each pick with its category and the fate of the boldest option.

**P-COL-25 Every option gets a hearing.** Give each option one recorded strength and one recorded concern before choosing. Test: The review record holds a strength and a concern for every option.

## Chapter 7: Building together

**P-COL-26 Write the brief first.** Write a short prose brief that states the change the work should make and for whom before making any visual. Test: A confirmed brief file exists and predates the first visual.

**P-COL-27 Make the brief deniable.** Write at least three this-not-that statements into the brief, and strike any sentence that would be true for every project. Test: The brief holds three or more explicit this-not-that exclusions.

**P-COL-28 Describe the feel first.** Describe how the result should feel in use, in sensory words, before choosing any visual. Test: The brief contains a sensory paragraph free of layout or component vocabulary.

**P-COL-29 Prototype the biggest risk.** Point each prototype at one named question about the largest risk, rated as value, usability, feasibility or viability. Test: Each prototype names its question and its risk type.

**P-COL-30 Fidelity follows the audience.** Keep artifacts rough inside the working conversation and raise fidelity for viewers outside it. Test: An artifact for an outside viewer can be understood without a verbal walkthrough.

**P-COL-31 Build both to end debates.** When two positions on a direction persist, build cheap versions of both and compare them instead of arguing. Test: Disagreements that lasted more than one exchange end in a recorded comparison, not a prose thread.
