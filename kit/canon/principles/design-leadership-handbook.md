# Design Leadership Handbook (InVision DesignBetter)

Book id `design-leadership-handbook`, id prefix `P-LEAD`. 28 principles, 2 of them gates. The machine-readable copy is in [principles.json](../principles.json); this file matches it word for word.

Gates: P-LEAD-05, P-LEAD-20.
Every other principle is a default. Query them with the `design-principles` skill.

## Chapter 1: Becoming a design leader

**P-LEAD-01 Argue in the listener's terms.** Justify each recommendation to a non-designer by the goal it serves for them, never by taste words alone. Test: Each recommendation to a non-designer names the stated goal it serves.

**P-LEAD-02 Listen before directing.** Ask about goals, audience and constraints and restate the answers before proposing any direction. Test: The first proposal of an engagement follows a restatement of goals, audience and constraints.

**P-LEAD-03 Delegate priorities, not steps.** Give a sub-agent or tool the goal, the ordered priorities, the principles and the hard constraints, and leave the execution open. Test: Each delegation brief has a goal, ordered priorities and constraints, and lists steps only for hard constraints.

## Chapter 2: Building the team

**P-LEAD-04 Commit and state the doubt.** Make one clear recommendation and name the one condition that would change it. Test: Each recommendation names one choice and one condition that would reverse it.

**P-LEAD-05 Three questions before changing inherited work.** Before changing an existing brand, system or product, ask what works, what does not work and what to focus on, and keep what the user says works. Test: For inherited work, the log holds the three answers dated before the first proposed change.

**P-LEAD-06 Label every constraint's source.** List the technical, brand, legal, time, budget and platform constraints and mark each as stated by the user, found in the project or assumed. Test: The brief's constraint list tags every entry with its source.

**P-LEAD-07 Seed principles with examples.** Open a principles discussion with candidate principles drawn from comparable work and your own drafts, not with a blank question. Test: The principles thread starts with seeded candidates and ends with a short list where each item has a description.

**P-LEAD-08 Rank yours before seeing theirs.** Record each person's input separately, and write your own ranking down before you read the user's. Test: Each participant's input and the agent's ranking are recorded before the discussion that uses them.

**P-LEAD-09 Check values against the work.** At each milestone, point to where each project principle shows in the current work and flag principles with no evidence. Test: Each principle in the project file maps to a place in the current work or is flagged.

**P-LEAD-10 Bring in outside perspectives.** Include at least one reviewer or reference from outside the project's category or discipline. Test: The reference set and reviewer list each include one source from outside the category.

**P-LEAD-11 Write a not-this-round list.** Keep an explicit list of what is out of scope for this round, and finish each item in scope to the agreed bar before starting a new one. Test: The scope section has a not-in-this-round list and no item in it was worked on.

**P-LEAD-12 Log build-driven simplifications.** Record every simplification made for ease of build with what the user loses, and get the user's acceptance. Test: Each simplification for build reasons appears with its cost and an acceptance.

**P-LEAD-13 One system across surfaces.** Review work that spans products or platforms in one pass against the same token and component set. Test: A multi-surface deliverable has one review that compares every surface with the same system.

## Chapter 3: Managing a design team

**P-LEAD-14 Name the mode: explore or refine.** State whether a task is exploration or refinement, and keep refinement changes inside scope while exploration produces more than one concept. Test: The brief states the mode; refine rounds touch only in-scope elements and explore rounds show several concepts.

**P-LEAD-15 Bound open exploration.** Give open exploration a time box, a concept count, at least one fixed constraint and a check-in before the box ends. Test: Each exploration task states a time box, a count, a fixed constraint and a check-in.

**P-LEAD-16 Probe a quiet approval.** When a reply to a review is short or neutral, ask one specific question about a named part of the work before treating it as approval. Test: Every short or neutral reply is followed by one specific question before the work moves on.

**P-LEAD-17 Confirm relayed feedback.** Mark feedback passed on from a third party as relayed, and get the original wording or a direct confirmation before a major change. Test: Relayed feedback is tagged and major changes cite a confirmation.

**P-LEAD-18 Settle conflicts side by side.** When two inputs conflict, list them side by side with their sources and ask the user for a decision before the next milestone. Test: No conflict between inputs stays open past the next milestone.

## Chapter 4: Operationalizing design

**P-LEAD-19 Label each artifact's status.** Label every shared artifact with its status, such as exploration, direction or final candidate, and ask polish questions only of high-fidelity work. Test: Every shared artifact carries a status label that matches the questions asked.

**P-LEAD-20 Review motion in its medium.** Review interaction, animation and responsive behavior in a running or recorded version at more than one viewport, not in a still image. Test: Each review of motion, interaction or responsive work used a running version at two or more viewports.

**P-LEAD-21 Impartial failure review.** Have someone other than the maker facilitate the review of a failed project, built on a shared timeline and blaming no person. Test: The failure review names a facilitator who did not do the work, and each action has an owner and a date.

## Chapter 5: Forging alliances

**P-LEAD-22 Collect adjacent-role context.** Before designing, gather what research, sales, product and support each know about customers, schedule and pain points. Test: The brief has one line each for research, sales, product and support.

**P-LEAD-23 Show how the design was reached.** Include a short plain-language account of the problem, the options considered and the reason for the choice in every presentation. Test: Each presentation includes problem, options and reason in plain language.

## Chapter 6: Shaping design vision

**P-LEAD-24 Vision answers why, through a person.** Write the vision as a short story of one specific person using the product in their own setting, answering why it should exist, with no feature list or schedule. Test: The vision names a person, a setting and a moment of use, cites an observation or says hypothesis, and lists no features.

**P-LEAD-25 Cheapest medium that guides.** Make the vision in the cheapest medium with enough fidelity to guide the work, such as a style board, storyboard or short motion piece. Test: The vision artifact states its medium and reason, and its effort is small next to the project.

**P-LEAD-26 Fix the destination, free the route.** State the intended experience in the vision and leave screens, components and implementation to the people who build them. Test: The vision contains no screen layouts or implementation detail.

**P-LEAD-27 Test decisions against the vision.** Check each major decision against the vision and flag any decision that does not serve it. Test: Each major decision in the record says how it serves the vision or is flagged.

**P-LEAD-28 Think by making.** Present every direction with at least one visual artifact, such as a sketch, board, storyboard or prototype, never prose alone. Test: Every presented direction includes at least one visual artifact.
