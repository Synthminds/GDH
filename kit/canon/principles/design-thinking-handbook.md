# Design Thinking Handbook (Design Better)

Book id `design-thinking-handbook`, id prefix `P-DT`. 32 principles, 6 of them gates. The machine-readable copy is in [principles.json](../principles.json); this file matches it word for word.

Gates: P-DT-10, P-DT-11, P-DT-13, P-DT-17, P-DT-21, P-DT-23.
Every other principle is a default. Query them with the `design-principles` skill.

## Chapter 1: Why we need design thinking

**P-DT-01 Say flare or focus.** State in every phase whether the work is widening the options or narrowing them, and do not narrow while you claim to be widening. Test: Every round header says flare or focus, and flare rounds add options rather than cut them.

**P-DT-02 Start where the project stands.** When a draft or product already exists, start by testing it as a prototype instead of restarting from research. Test: The plan names its entry phase and the reason for it.

**P-DT-03 Build sure, test doubtful.** Make each prototype as if you are right, then test it as if you are wrong. Test: Each test plan states the result that would disprove the direction.

## Chapter 2: Empathize

**P-DT-04 First idea is a hypothesis.** Record the first solution idea, the user's or yours, as a hypothesis with at least one observation that would disprove it. Test: The brief lists the initial idea with a disconfirming observation to look for.

**P-DT-05 Get close to real use.** Collect at least one source from the real setting of use, such as a screen recording, photos of the place, or a walk-through. Test: The research notes include at least one source captured in the real context.

**P-DT-06 Hunt workarounds and extreme users.** Ask who uses the product far more, far less or in an odd way, and what workarounds people invent, before defining the need. Test: The notes list at least one workaround or extreme-user behavior, or record that none was found.

**P-DT-07 Record fears and delights.** Describe the people served with at least one fear and one moment of delight tied to the situation, not only their tasks. Test: The user description names at least one fear and one delight.

**P-DT-08 Shrink research, never skip.** When time is short, run the single cheapest empathy activity that fits, such as one interview or one walk-through, instead of skipping research. Test: A constrained project still shows one empathy activity and its findings.

**P-DT-09 Patterns before problem statements.** Group raw observations into named patterns and write one insight per pattern before defining the problem. Test: A synthesis note groups observations under named patterns with linked insights.

## Chapter 3: Define

**P-DT-10 State the need without the product.** Write the need so it still makes sense after every product, channel and format word is removed. Test: Removing every product noun from the need statement leaves a sentence that still makes sense.

**P-DT-11 Name the target feeling.** End the point of view with how the person should feel when the need is met. Test: The point of view or brief contains a clause naming the target feeling.

**P-DT-12 Reframe before you ideate.** Write at least two framings of the problem, including one zoomed out to a wider goal and one zoomed in to the sharpest moment, and compare the directions each opens. Test: The record shows two or more framings and a reason for the chosen one.

**P-DT-13 Name who it is not for.** State in the brief who the work is not for and whose need loses when two groups want opposite things. Test: The brief names at least one group the work is not for.

**P-DT-14 Map before and after.** Map the experience from before the person meets the design to after they leave it, and mark which touchpoints the project can change. Test: A journey list covers before, during and after with each touchpoint marked changeable or fixed.

**P-DT-15 POV must pick a winner.** Test the point of view by applying it to two competing ideas, and sharpen it until it favors one of them. Test: Applied to two sample ideas, the point of view favors one with a stated reason.

## Chapter 4: Ideate

**P-DT-16 Questions from the POV.** Derive several how-might-we questions from phrases of the point of view, each narrower than the whole problem and none naming a solution. Test: Each question traces to a phrase of the point of view and names no solution.

**P-DT-17 Generate first, judge later.** Keep evaluation and feasibility comments out of the generation phase entirely. Test: No evaluation or feasibility remark appears in the generation phase of the round.

**P-DT-18 Quantity target and time box.** Give each ideation topic a time box and a numeric idea target, and report the actual count. Test: Each round states its time box and target and reports the count reached.

**P-DT-19 Headline every idea.** Give each idea a few-word headline and a thumbnail or one-line visual description, and share one idea at a time. Test: Every idea in the round has a headline and a visual description.

**P-DT-20 Select across several criteria.** Pick ideas under several named criteria, such as easiest win, most delightful and long shot, without cutting for feasibility first. Test: The selection shows a pick per criterion and no idea was cut for feasibility beforehand.

**P-DT-21 Never carry only the safest.** Take two or more ideas into prototyping, and make sure at least one is not the lowest-risk choice. Test: Two or more ideas enter prototyping and at least one is not the lowest-risk option.

**P-DT-22 Keep the wild idea's appeal.** For each wild idea you reject, write what is attractive in it and look for a buildable way to keep that quality. Test: Each rejected wild idea has a one-line note on its attractive quality.

## Chapter 5: Prototype

**P-DT-23 Question and decision rule first.** Before building a prototype, write the question it answers and the result that would change the plan. Test: Every prototype has a written question and a plan-changing result.

**P-DT-24 Time-box two or three variants.** Build two or three low-resolution variations inside a short time box before investing in one. Test: The round states its time box and produced two or three variations.

**P-DT-25 Fake the hard part by hand.** Simulate the costly part of a product, such as an algorithm or a service, by hand behind the interface while you still learn what people need. Test: The prototype brief lists every hand-simulated part.

**P-DT-26 Polish can be function.** Do not mark feedback cues, transitions or other polish optional until you have tested what they do. Test: Every delayed or changing state in the prototype has a tested feedback cue.

**P-DT-27 Test symbols per locale.** Test icons, labels and key terms with each target audience and locale before build. Test: The test plan lists each locale or audience with its checked icons and terms.

**P-DT-28 Demonstrate, don't chart.** To make one point clear, build the simplest demonstration that shows it and strip anything that carries no data. Test: Each key message has one plain demonstration or view with no decoration that carries no data.

## Chapter 6: Test

**P-DT-29 Show before you explain.** Present the artifact and a task first, and explain intent only after the reaction. Test: The first message of a review presents the artifact and a task with no rationale.

**P-DT-30 Bring two to every test.** Show at least two options in a test and record the comparison. Test: Each test shows two or more options and records the comparison.

**P-DT-31 Tag every claim's evidence.** Tag each claim about users in the brief as observed, reported or assumed. Test: Every claim about users in the brief carries an evidence tag.

**P-DT-32 Review harm and misuse.** Write a short review of how the design could harm, shame or be misused by the people who use it. Test: The brief contains a harm and misuse review.
