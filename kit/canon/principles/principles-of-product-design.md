# Principles of Product Design (InVision DesignBetter)

Book id `principles-of-product-design`, id prefix `P-PPD`. 39 principles, 10 of them gates. The machine-readable copy is in [principles.json](../principles.json); this file matches it word for word.

Gates: P-PPD-01, P-PPD-07, P-PPD-13, P-PPD-19, P-PPD-20, P-PPD-22, P-PPD-23, P-PPD-24, P-PPD-31, P-PPD-34.
Every other principle is a default. Query them with the `design-principles` skill.

## Chapter 1: Guess less

**P-PPD-01 Label evidence and assumptions.** List the evidence behind each major design decision, such as audience, primary task and feature set, and label every decision without evidence as an assumption. Test: Every key decision in the brief points to an evidence item or carries the label assumption.

**P-PPD-02 Mine existing data first.** Ask for and read the data that already exists, such as analytics, support themes, sales notes and survey results, before asking for new research. Test: The brief lists each existing-data source type with found or none available.

**P-PPD-03 Use the users' own words.** Collect the words users use for the product and their tasks, and use those words in labels, headings and calls to action. Test: Labels and headings in the deliverable use terms from the user-language list, not internal names.

**P-PPD-04 Ask why people switch.** For a redesign, find out why people adopt or abandon the product and decide whether the problem is function or perception. Test: A redesign brief names its why-people-switch source and calls the problem function or perception.

**P-PPD-05 Keep a feedback channel running.** Name one feedback source that keeps running after launch and where its results land. Test: The handoff names one continuous feedback source and its destination.

## Chapter 2: Story first

**P-PPD-06 Write the product story first.** Write a short story of how the product fits into one person's life before designing any screen. Test: A dated story exists before the first layout and answers how the product fits a person's life.

**P-PPD-07 Keep interface out of stories.** Keep screens, components and technology out of the product story and describe only people, context, need and value. Test: The story artifact contains no layout, component or technology names.

**P-PPD-08 Ground stories in research.** Base every character and plot point in the story on a research item or a user statement, and mark invented ones as assumptions. Test: Each character and plot point links to a source or carries the label assumption.

**P-PPD-09 Shape journeys as story arcs.** Lay a user journey on a story arc from setup and trigger through rising trouble, crisis and climax to resolution, with the user's words at each stage. Test: The journey map has the arc stages in order, each with a thought or quote and its hurdles and channels.

**P-PPD-10 Storyboard the user's feelings.** Draw each storyboard panel with its setting, the action and the user's feeling at that step. Test: Every storyboard panel states setting, action and feeling.

**P-PPD-11 Gate video stories by scope.** Produce a video story only for a new product, a long project or a cross-channel experience, and keep detailed interface out of its script. Test: The plan records which of the three conditions justifies a vision video before one is made.

**P-PPD-12 Write job stories in context.** Write each need as a job story: when a situation occurs, the person wants a motivation, so they can reach an outcome. Test: Each job story has all three parts and cites the statement it came from.

## Chapter 3: Pencils before pixels

**P-PPD-13 Explore at the lowest fidelity.** Explore with text outlines, flow lists, grey boxes, thumbnails and rough palettes before producing any polished output. Test: The log shows a reviewed low-fidelity step before the first high-fidelity artifact.

**P-PPD-14 Many rough concepts, then narrow.** Generate many rough concepts that differ in structure or approach, not only in color or font, before selecting the directions to develop. Test: At least the stated number of structurally distinct rough concepts exists before selection.

**P-PPD-15 Limit the tool, not the idea.** Set a hard limit on each exploration round, such as greyscale only, a fixed time or a fixed element count, so detail cannot creep in. Test: Each exploration round states its limit and contains no final copy, color or imagery.

**P-PPD-16 Answer vagueness with a sketch.** When a request is abstract, answer with one or two quick concrete interpretations and ask which is closer before investing in a design. Test: Abstract requests receive a concrete rough guess and a confirm question before full work.

**P-PPD-17 Mark drafts as drafts.** Show concept work in a visibly unfinished form with a draft label when you want feedback on the idea. Test: Concept-review artifacts carry a draft label and no final styling.

## Chapter 4: Show and tell

**P-PPD-18 Review early, mid and late.** Schedule at least three reviews per project, with the first one before any high-fidelity work. Test: The plan shows three or more review points and the first predates high-fidelity work.

**P-PPD-19 Never stage a big reveal.** Let no more than one fidelity step pass between user checkpoints, so the first view of the design is never the final deliverable. Test: No fidelity level is skipped between two user checkpoints.

**P-PPD-20 Frame then ask specific questions.** Open every review with the user goal, the business goal and the constraints, then ask the specific questions you need answered. Test: Each review request begins with the three framing items and lists explicit questions.

**P-PPD-21 Frame, then stop pitching.** Show the work after the framing without persuasion or a walkthrough of how to read it, and record feedback before rebutting any of it. Test: The text before the artifact holds only framing and questions, and no feedback is rebutted in the turn it arrives.

**P-PPD-22 Critique ties element to goal.** Write each critique item as an element, an observation and the goal it helps or harms, never a bare like or dislike. Test: Every logged critique item names an element, an observation and an affected goal.

**P-PPD-23 Critique the work, not maker.** Make the design the subject of every critique sentence, never the person who made it. Test: No critique sentence has the maker as its subject.

**P-PPD-24 Problems before solutions.** List what works and what needs refinement first, and offer fixes only in a separate optional block. Test: Each critique has non-empty working-well and needs-refinement lists, with fixes kept separate.

**P-PPD-25 Separate maker and critic.** Keep the role that made the work apart from the role that critiques it, and log all feedback for the maker afterwards. Test: The review record names maker and critic as different roles, or is labelled single-context.

**P-PPD-26 Retrospect into harness changes.** After each project, record what worked, what did not and what to change, and turn each change into a proposed edit to a skill, rule or catalog. Test: A closed project has a retrospective with three lists and at least one proposed harness change or a reason for none.

## Chapter 5: Fast feedback

**P-PPD-27 Prototype only open questions.** Scope each prototype to the parts you have the most questions about and stub the rest. Test: Every screen or flow in the prototype serves a listed open question.

**P-PPD-28 Rough for peers, real for users.** Keep concept prototypes rough for colleagues, but make prototypes for end-user tests realistic enough that the tester forgets it is a prototype. Test: An end-user test prototype has realistic content, runs on the target device and contains no filler.

**P-PPD-29 Cheap enough to throw away.** Keep each prototype cheap enough that you will still change or discard it when feedback demands. Test: The prototype's build cost is recorded and no paid generation precedes a tested direction.

**P-PPD-30 Check feasibility before users.** Get a builder's feasibility check, covering data availability, required fields and platform limits, before putting a prototype in front of users. Test: A feasibility note exists and predates the first user test.

**P-PPD-31 Show it on the target device.** Review and test the prototype on the device class and viewport it is meant for, not only on a desktop canvas. Test: Review captures come from the target viewport or device class.

**P-PPD-32 Small rounds, repeated.** Test with a small group of users per round, revise, and test again rather than running one large round. Test: Each test round has a small participant count and a logged revision before the next round.

**P-PPD-33 Script tests, never help.** Run user tests from a written script with welcome, warm-up, introduction, tasks and debrief, and give no hints during tasks. Test: A five-part script exists and the session notes show no hints during tasks.

## Chapter 6: Lateral design

**P-PPD-34 Confirm data fields exist.** Before high-fidelity design of any data-driven view or form, confirm that every displayed field exists and is available, and get the required field list from the code or its owner. Test: Every field shown in the design appears in a confirmed field list.

**P-PPD-35 Flows follow user tasks.** Name navigation and flows after user tasks from the job stories, not after tables, endpoints or data models. Test: Navigation and flow names match user tasks, and none is a table or endpoint name.

**P-PPD-36 Write named design values.** Write a short named list of design values for the project and cite one by name whenever a contested choice is defended. Test: Each contested decision in the record cites a named design value.

**P-PPD-37 Pair maker with critic.** Run substantial design work as a pair: a lead maker and a partner who critiques and unsticks. Test: Each substantial task names a lead and a partner or critic.

## Chapter 7: Break the black box

**P-PPD-38 Keep work visibly in progress.** Keep work in progress in a shared place the user can open at any time, with versions and notes, not only the latest polished state. Test: The project folder holds every round in order with notes and rejected options.

**P-PPD-39 Seek feedback, keep the decision.** Collect feedback from anyone, then give each item a decision of accepted, rejected or deferred with a reason tied to a goal, value or evidence. Test: Every feedback item has a recorded decision and reason, and no change cites only that someone asked.
