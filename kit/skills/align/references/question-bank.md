# Question bank

Questions for each topic of the full interview, in tree order. Ask them in the form set in `interview-loop.md`: what you found, a recommended answer, its reason. Skip a question when a file answers it or the depth table skips its topic.

Each question shows:

- A tag. **Book**: the question or method is in the named source. **Adapted**: built from a method or a case in the source, worded for one user in a chat. **Derived**: GDH's own; no source holds it.
- Source keys: DT Design Thinking Handbook; DE Designing for Emotion; DC Design Collaboration Handbook; DO DesignOps Handbook; DL Design Leadership Handbook; PD Principles of Product Design.
- `Rec:` the recommended answer to build. `Weak:` the answer pattern that is not usable yet. `Probe:` the follow-up to ask.
- The brief section the answer fills.

All wording here is GDH's own. The sources are named, not quoted. Reuse no statistic from a source.

## 1. People served and context

**1.1 Who is the one person this must work for first? Give their role, their situation and one thing they did last week that touches this.** [Book, DT ch. 3] Fills 2 User.
- Rec: the most specific person the files support, with two concrete details.
- Weak: "everyone", "small businesses", an age band. Probe: "Name one real person who fits. What did they do last week that relates?"

**1.2 Who else touches it: who pays, approves, sets it up, or is in the room when it is used?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 3] Fills 2 Also touched by.
- Rec: the buyer and the approver, when they differ from the user.
- Weak: "just the customer". Probe: "Who explains it to someone else? Who can say no to buying it?"

**1.3 Who is this not for?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3; DE ch. 3] Fills 2 Not for.
- Rec: the group whose needs pull against the person in 1.1.
- Weak: "we exclude nobody". Probe: "When two groups want opposite things, whose need wins?"

**1.4 Where and in what state do they meet it: device, place, time pressure, distraction, connection?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6] Fills 2 Context.
- Rec: the context that 1.1 implies, such as "phone, one hand, in a hurry".
- Weak: "at a desk, I suppose". Probe: "Standing or sitting? How many seconds do they have? What competes for attention?"

**1.5 What happens just before and just after? Where does the experience really start?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3] Fills 2 Context.
- Rec: the step before the first touchpoint and the step after the last.
- Weak: "it starts on the home page". Probe: "What made them come? What did they look up first? What do they do next?"

**1.6 What have you seen them do, what were you told, and what do you assume?** [Derived] Fills evidence tags in 2 and 15.
- Rec: tag each claim about people observed, reported or assumed.
- Weak: "I know my customers". Probe: "Name one thing you watched a customer do. Which belief about them was never checked?"

**1.7 Do they differ by language, culture, device or network quality?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5] Fills 2 Context and 9 Content and languages.
- Rec: the languages in the content and the weakest device the files suggest.
- Weak: "it is the same everywhere". Probe: "Which regions? What phone and network does the least-equipped person have?"

## 2. Need and point of view

**2.1 What is the person trying to get done, and what need sits under it?** [Book, DT ch. 3] Fills 3 Need.
- Rec: a verb phrase with no product noun in it.
- Weak: a feature, such as "they need a dashboard". Probe: "What would it let them do or feel that they cannot now? What for?"

**2.2 Walk me through the last time this happened, step by step.** [Derived] Fills 3 Evidence.
- Rec: one dated occasion, told in order.
- Weak: "usually they just...". Probe: "Pick one occasion. What came first? Then what?"

**2.3 What do they do today instead?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2] Fills 3 Evidence.
- Rec: the workaround the files or the user mention.
- Weak: "nothing, that is the problem". Probe: "They cope somehow: a sheet, a friend, a rival product. Which one?"

**2.4 What surprised you about these people?** [Book, DT ch. 3, the point-of-view fill-in] Fills 3 Insight.
- Rec: a finding a newcomer to the field would not guess.
- Weak: an obvious fact. Probe: "Would a newcomer already guess that? What did you not expect?"

**2.5 Here are two framings of the problem. Which opens the better set of solutions?** [Book, DT ch. 3, reframing] Fills 3 Framings.
- Rec: framing A as the request states it; framing B with the format removed, or one level wider or narrower. Name the directions each opens.
- Weak: "both are fine". Probe: "Which one rules out an idea you like? That one decides more."

**2.6 Does the problem statement name a solution or a format? Say it again without one.** [Adapted, DT ch. 3] Fills 3 Framings.
- Rec: the statement with the format noun replaced by the outcome.
- Weak: "we need a new website". Probe: "If websites did not exist, what would still need to happen for this person?"

**2.7 What did you first think the answer was, and what would prove it wrong?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2] Fills 15 as a hypothesis.
- Rec: the first idea in the request, with one observation that would disprove it.
- Weak: "it is obvious". Probe: "If it were built perfectly and nothing improved, what would the reason be?"

**2.8 Does this hold: "<user> needs a way to <need>, because <insight>"?** [Book, DT ch. 3] Fills 3 Point of view.
- Rec: the statement built from 1.1, 2.1 and 2.4.
- Weak: a yes with no edit. Probe: "Which word would you change? Could it choose between two ideas we might try?"

## 3. Feel

**3.1 How should they feel right after using this?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 3] Fills 4 Target feeling.
- Rec: one word, such as relieved, proud, in control, calm or curious, with a reason from 2.
- Weak: "satisfied", "happy". Probe: "Pick one from the list. Why that one?"

**3.2 What must they never feel here?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 3] Fills 4 Must not feel.
- Rec: the feeling that the worry in 3.3 produces.
- Weak: "nothing bad". Probe: "What would make them feel stupid, rushed or watched?"

**3.3 What worries them most at this step?** [Book, DT ch. 2] Fills 4 and feeds 10.
- Rec: the worry the support messages or the request show.
- Weak: "they want it to be easy". Probe: "What is the worst outcome they picture? What burned them before?"

**3.4 What delights them today, here or nearby?** [Book, DT ch. 2] Fills 4 Delight to keep.
- Rec: a moment worth keeping or amplifying.
- Weak: "not much". Probe: "When did one last thank you or tell a friend? What had just happened?"

**3.5 Describe the experience in sensory words: pace, warmth, weight, sound. No layout words.** [Adapted, DC ch. 7] Fills 4 Sensory line.
- Rec: a short line such as "unhurried, warm, solid underfoot".
- Weak: layout words, such as "a big hero". Probe: "If it were a room, how would it feel to walk in?"

## 4. Primary job and success

**4.1 After using this, what has that person done? One verb and one object.** [Derived] Fills 5 Primary job.
- Rec: one job such as "booked a first appointment". Rank any others 2 and 3.
- Weak: two jobs at once. Probe: "If only one could work perfectly, which?"

**4.2 What will people do that shows it worked, and how will we see it?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6; DC ch. 5] Fills 5 Do measure.
- Rec: one behavior and the way to observe it. Ask for the baseline, or write "baseline unknown".
- Weak: "more engagement". Probe: "Which action, by whom, by when, compared with what today?"

**4.3 What should people feel, and how would we notice?** [Book, DC ch. 5] Fills 5 Feel measure.
- Rec: the feeling from 3.1 turned into a test, such as testers naming it unprompted.
- Weak: "they will love it". Probe: "What would a tester say or do that shows the feeling?"

**4.4 What would you love to hear one of them say afterwards?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3] Fills 5 Story.
- Rec: one sentence in the user's words about their own life.
- Weak: "that it is great". Probe: "In their words. What exact sentence?"

**4.5 What does the owner or the organization need from this?** [Adapted, DT ch. 1; DC ch. 2 and 3] Fills 5 Owner need.
- Rec: the owner's measure, and the design decision that serves it.
- Weak: "growth". Probe: "Which number, and how does this piece of work move it?"

**4.6 Could this win praise and still fail its users? How would we notice?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3] Fills 5 and feeds 10.
- Rec: the group that must succeed without help.
- Weak: "if it looks good, it is good". Probe: "Who must use it without help? Where would they struggle?"

## 5. Design persona

`design-brief` holds the persona form and its checks. Ask one field at a time. Offer two or three drafts for each field. The short form asks 5.1, 5.3, 5.5 with three traits, and 5.6.

**5.1 If this product were a person in the room, who would it be?** [Book, DE ch. 3] Fills 6 opening answer, word for word.
- Rec: a character drawn from the feeling in 3.1 and the people in 1.
- Weak: a celebrity name with no reason. Probe: "What does that person do that the product should do?"

**5.2 What is the product, brand or service called?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 1] Fills 6 Name. Look it up first.

**5.3 Who is it as a person, and what makes it unlike others in its category?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 2] Fills 6 Character.
- Rec: two to four sentences that fail the swap with a named competitor.
- Weak: "friendly and professional". Probe: "Could this describe <competitor>? Then what is different?"

**5.4 Which person, character or mascot carries this character, and why?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 3] Fills 6 Stand-in. Internal reference only.

**5.5 Name five to seven traits as "X, but not Y". The first three are the brand words. Name one trait it never has.** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 4] Fills 6 Traits.
- Rec: traits from 3 and 7.3, each with a limit that names a real risk of that trait.
- Weak: a trait with no limit, or a limit that is its opposite. Probe: "Where does that trait go too far?"

**5.6 How does it speak, and how does the voice shift when the user succeeds, errs, is anxious, or when the product fails?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 5] Fills 6 Voice.
- Rec: humor falls and plainness rises as the situation gets worse.
- Weak: one tone for all cases. Probe: "Would it joke during an outage?"

**5.7 What are the real success, user error, product failure and empty-state messages?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 6] Fills 6 Real strings. Draft them; the user edits.

**5.8 What color, type and style intent follows from each trait? Words only.** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 7] Fills 6 Visual lexicon. No hex value and no font file.

**5.9 Which one or two peaks do we own, and which low point needs care?** [Book, DE ch. 3, field 8] Fills 6 Moments.

## 6. Brand, existing system and assets

**6.1 Here is what I found: <logo, colors, type, voice, components, imagery>. What is missing or out of date?** [Derived] Fills 7 Inventory.
- Rec: the inventory from the look-up, each item with its file.
- Weak: "we have a logo". Probe: "Please share the files and any guide. What is used in practice, not only in the guide?"

**6.2 Which of these are fixed, and which may change?** [Derived] Fills 7 Fixed or flexible.
- Rec: mark each item fixed, flexible or unknown. A fixed item is brand-required in later reviews.
- Weak: "whatever you think". Probe: "If I changed the logo color, who would object?"

**6.3 Where does the current experience break the promise the brand makes?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6] Fills 7 Gap.
- Rec: the moment the support messages complain about most.
- Weak: "it is fine, just dated". Probe: "Which moment feels least like you?"

**6.4 What real content and data can I use: copy, product images, numbers?** [Derived] Fills 7 Content and 9.
- Rec: design with real content. A missing item becomes a named placeholder, never an invented fact.
- Weak: "use placeholder text". Probe: "Give me one real headline and one real data set."

**6.5 What was tried before, by you or anyone else, and what happened?** [Book, DT ch. 4, client questions] Fills 7 History.
- Rec: earlier work found in the repository or the decision record.
- Weak: "nothing much". Probe: "Any earlier design, agency or test? Why was it dropped?"

## 7. Taste

**7.1 Show me two or three things you admire, from any field. Which exact part, and why does it work?** [Derived] Fills 8 References.
- Rec: ask for the part: type, color, density, imagery, layout, motion or voice. You write the attribute to carry.
- Weak: "clean and modern". Probe: "Point at the part you mean. Now show one that others call clean and you dislike."

**7.2 Show me one or two things you want to stay away from. Which part, and why?** [Derived] Fills 8 References.
- Rec: the same two follow-ups. You write the attribute to avoid.
- Weak: "I will know it when I see it". Probe: "Which competitor would you never want to be mistaken for?"

**7.3 Which three words should someone use after one minute with it?** [Adapted, DE ch. 3] Fills 8 Brand words.
- Rec: words drawn from the people and the job. Avoid words every project claims.
- Weak: "clean, modern, simple". Probe: "Keep the word. What would it look like if it went too far?"

**7.4 For each word: what does it mean here, and what does it not mean?** [Adapted, DE ch. 3] Fills 8 Brand words.
- Rec: offer two concrete readings. You write "shows up as" and confirm it.
- Weak: a definition with no limit. Probe: "What would be too much of it?"

**7.5 Who will this sit next to in the user's mind?** [Derived] Fills 8 Competitors, for the swap and contrast tests.

**7.6 Which convention of the category must we keep, and which cliche must we avoid?** [Derived] Fills 8 Conventions.
- Weak: "just make it stand out". Probe: "What would make a customer doubt you are a real <category>?"

**7.7 Is your taste the same as your audience's taste? How do you know?** [Derived] Fills 8 and 15.
- Weak: "they like what I like". Probe: "Which choice of yours has a customer ever questioned?"

**7.8 Here are three "this, not that" lines. Which is wrong?** [Book, DC ch. 7] Fills 8 This, not that.
- Rec: three trade-offs you draft from the answers so far. A corrected line is the most useful one.

**7.9 What excites you most about this project?** [Book, DT ch. 4, client questions] Fills 8 and 12.
- Weak: "getting it done". Probe: "If one part could be remarkable, which part?"

## 8. Constraints

**8.1 What must we say, never say, or be able to prove?** [Derived] Fills 9 Legal and claims.
- Rec: "no claims beyond what you give me". This is intake, not legal advice; write "confirm with counsel" where unsure.

**8.2 Which accessibility level must this meet? Is there a named law, policy or customer requirement?** [Derived] Fills 9 Accessibility level.
- Rec: WCAG 2.2 AA, labelled DOCUMENTED. Record a named policy by name only. The kernel floor applies whatever the answer.

**8.3 What will it be built in, where must it run, and who builds it?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5; DL ch. 2] Fills 9 Tech stack.
- Rec: what the manifest shows. Ask about the oldest device, offline use, and whether motion, 3D or video is allowed.
- Weak: "standard". Probe: "Oldest device? Worst network? Who writes the code?"

**8.4 When is it needed, and what fixes that date?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 3] Fills 9 Deadline.
- Weak: "as soon as possible". Probe: "Which date is set by something outside your control? What happens if we miss it?"

**8.5 What can you not do?** [Book, DT ch. 4, client questions] Fills 9.
- Weak: "nothing is off the table". Probe: "Technology, staff, law, contracts, money: one limit in each."

**8.6 What will you not do, even though you could?** [Book, DT ch. 4, client questions] Fills 9 and 8.
- Weak: "I am open to anything". Probe: "A competitor does this. Would you?"

## 9. Risks and the worst case

**9.1 What is the worst outcome of this design, and for whom?** [Book, DC ch. 4] Fills 10 Worst case.
- Rec: one named group, one harm, and a response: design change, test, or accepted risk.
- Weak: "nothing bad". Probe: "Who is most vulnerable among the users? What if they misread the key screen?"

**9.2 How might people misuse it, or use only half of it?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2] Fills 10 Misuse.
- Weak: "they will follow the instructions". Probe: "What shortcut would a tired person take?"

**9.3 What could make someone feel shame, fear or a loss of control?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 3] Fills 10.
- Weak: "it is just a tool". Probe: "What does using it say about the person? Who might see them use it?"

**9.4 Which word, icon, color or symbol could some of the audience read differently?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5] Fills 10 and feeds feedback tests.
- Weak: "it is universal". Probe: "Has anyone outside the team, in each market, seen it?"

**9.5 Which assumption, if wrong, ends the project? What is the cheapest check?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5] Fills 10 Riskiest assumption.
- Weak: "we are confident". Probe: "List what must be true for this to work. Which item has no evidence?"

**9.6 What happens under bad conditions: a slow network, a small screen, stress?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5 and 6] Fills 10.
- Weak: "that is an edge case". Probe: "How often does it happen to your least-equipped user?"

## 10. Decision rights

**10.1 Who makes the final call on this work?** [Derived] Fills 11 Decider.
- Weak: "the team". Probe: "If two people disagree, whose view stands?"

**10.2 For each aspect, who decides: scope, look, copy, brand, budget, launch?** [Adapted, DO ch. 2; DC ch. 3] Fills 11 Decider table.
- Rec: one decider for each aspect. The agent decides execution details inside the brief.
- Weak: "we all decide". Probe: "Name one person for each row."

**10.3 Who else can approve or veto, and when should they see the work?** [Adapted, DO ch. 2; DT ch. 4] Fills 11 Approvers.
- Weak: "I will show them at the end". Probe: "Is there anyone who can say no at the end? Can they see an early round?"

**10.4 What may I decide without asking you?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3] Fills 11 Delegated.
- Weak: "use your judgement". Probe: "Color, copy, layout, scope: which need your sign-off each time?"

**10.5 When we disagree on an open question, may I build both options and compare them?** [Adapted, DT ch. 5] Fills 11 Disputes.

**10.6 When evidence from users contradicts your preference, which wins?** [Adapted, DT ch. 2 and 6] Fills 11 Disputes.
- Weak: "my customers and I agree". Probe: "Suppose a test shows they pick the option you dislike. What then?"

## 11. Scope, out of scope and fidelity

**11.1 What do you need in hand at the end, in rank order with no ties?** [Book, DO ch. 2] Fills 12 Deliverables.
- Rec: a ranked list with one item that can finish in the first session.
- Weak: "everything is high priority". Probe: "If only one could ship this week, which?"

**11.2 What is out of this round?** [Book, DL ch. 2] Fills 12 Not in this round.
- Rec: the items the ranking put last, and every idea outside the job.

**11.3 Are we exploring a new look, or refining the one that exists?** [Book, DL ch. 3] Fills 12 Mode.
- Rec: explore with no design system; refine when one exists and the request says improve, fix or polish.

**11.4 What question must the first thing you see answer?** [Book, DT ch. 5] Fills 12 First question.
- Weak: "whether it is good". Probe: "Good at what, for whom? What result would change our direction?"

**11.5 How finished should the first thing be, and where does this engagement stop?** [Book, DT ch. 5; PD ch. 3] Fills 12 Fidelity.
- Rec: the lowest rung of the fidelity ladder that still has open questions. `design-brief` holds the ladder.

**11.6 What is the simplest example of this problem?** [Book, DT ch. 5] Fills 12 First scope.
- Weak: "it is all connected". Probe: "One person, one task, one screen. Which?"

**11.7 In what format does it end, and who receives it with what tool?** [Derived] Fills 12 Format.

## 12. Tools, spend budget and exposure

**12.1 May this project use tools that cost credits?** [Derived] Fills 13 Spend budget.
- Rec: "ask me before each spend call", because every cost is then seen first. Other options: none; up to a ceiling in the user's own unit.
- Say that each spend call still shows a quote and waits for a yes. Quote no price from memory.

**12.2 What may leave the project: uploads of your files to a tool, share links, previews, deploys, public posts?** [Derived] Fills 13 Exposure.
- Rec: "ask me each time". Record named exceptions, such as "preview deploys are fine".

**12.3 This session holds these design tools: <list>. Which do you want used or avoided?** [Derived] Fills 13 Tools.
- Rec: the list from the presence check. Make no tool call to find out.

**12.4 Are any files private or client-owned, never to be uploaded anywhere?** [Derived] Fills 13 Exposure.

## 13. How feedback rounds run

**13.1 Who reacts to the work: only you, or real users too, and in their real setting?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6] Fills 14 Who reacts.
- Weak: "I will decide for them". Probe: "Can one real user look at round two, on their own device?"

**13.2 For each round, what do we test and what do we want to know?** [Book, DT ch. 6] Fills 14 Round question.
- Rec: each round opens with one stated question.

**13.3 Will you try each thing before I explain it, and give your first reaction before a verdict?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6] Fills 14 Conduct.
- Weak: "explain it first". Probe: "Your customer gets no explanation. May I see where you get stuck first?"

**13.4 How often do you want an update?** [Adapted, DT ch. 6] Fills 14 Cadence.
- Rec: a short note after each round.

**13.5 In an idea round, may we hold judgement until the round ends?** [Book, DT ch. 4] Fills 14 Conduct.

**13.6 What kind of finding sends us back to rewrite the point of view?** [Adapted, DT ch. 3 and 6] Fills 14 Reframe trigger.

**13.7 Do you want more or fewer options from me, and more or less direction?** [Adapted, DL ch. 3] Fills 14 Working style.
